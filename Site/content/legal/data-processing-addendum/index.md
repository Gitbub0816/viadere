This Data Processing Addendum (the "**DPA**") forms part of the [Business Services Agreement](/legal/business-services-agreement/) (the "**Agreement**") between [COUNSEL: LEGAL ENTITY NAME] ("**Viadere**") and the business that accepted the Agreement ("**Customer**"). It applies where Viadere processes Customer Personal Data (defined below) on Customer's behalf in providing the Services. Terms used but not defined here have the meaning given in the Agreement.

## 1. Definitions

- "**Applicable Data Protection Law**" means all laws applicable to the processing of Customer Personal Data, including (as applicable) the EU General Data Protection Regulation 2016/679 ("**GDPR**"), the UK GDPR and the UK Data Protection Act 2018, the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act and its regulations ("**CCPA**"), and other US state privacy laws, and Canadian federal and provincial privacy laws.
- "**Controller**", "**Processor**", "**Data Subject**", "**Personal Data**", "**Personal Data Breach**", "**Processing**", "**Supervisory Authority**" have the meanings in the GDPR, and include the equivalent terms under other Applicable Data Protection Law, and "**Business**", "**Service Provider**", "**Contractor**", "**Sell**", "**Share**" and "**Consumer**" have the meanings in the CCPA.
- "**Customer Personal Data**" means Personal Data that Viadere processes on behalf of Customer in connection with the Services, as described in Annex I. It does not include Personal Data that Viadere processes as an independent Controller (section 2.4).
- "**EEA**" means the European Economic Area. "**Restricted Transfer**" means a transfer of Customer Personal Data that is subject to Applicable Data Protection Law of the EEA, UK or Switzerland to a country that has not been recognized as providing an adequate level of protection.
- "**SCCs**" means the standard contractual clauses annexed to Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914. "**UK Addendum**" means the International Data Transfer Addendum to the SCCs issued by the UK Information Commissioner under section 119A of the Data Protection Act 2018, version B1.0.
- "**Security Incident**" means a breach of security leading to the accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorized disclosure of or access to Customer Personal Data in Viadere's or its subprocessors' possession or control. It does not include unsuccessful attempts or activities that do not compromise security, such as unsuccessful log-in attempts, pings, port scans, denial-of-service attacks that do not lead to access, and similar events.
- "**Subprocessor**" means a third party engaged by Viadere to process Customer Personal Data.

## 2. Scope and roles

### 2.1 Processor and Controller

For Customer Personal Data, Customer is the Controller (or Business) and Viadere is the Processor (or Service Provider). Customer is responsible for determining the purposes and means of processing, and Viadere processes the data only as this DPA and Customer's documented instructions require. Where Customer is itself a Processor for another Controller, Customer represents that its instructions and its authorization of Viadere, including of the Subprocessors, are authorized by the relevant Controller, and Viadere is a sub-processor.

### 2.2 Details of processing

The subject matter, duration, nature and purpose of the processing, the types of Personal Data and the categories of Data Subjects are in Annex I.

### 2.3 Customer's obligations

Customer will: (a) comply with Applicable Data Protection Law in its use of the Services, including by providing notices and obtaining consents and having a lawful basis for the processing it instructs; (b) give instructions that comply with law; (c) be responsible for the accuracy, quality and lawfulness of the Personal Data and the means by which it acquired it; and (d) not send to Viadere special categories of data or any other data that the Services are not designed to process, except as Annex I or an addendum allows.

### 2.4 Viadere as independent Controller

Viadere is an independent Controller, and not Customer's Processor, for the following, which it processes for its own purposes and under its [Privacy Notice](/legal/privacy-notice/): (a) Consumer accounts, passkeys, devices, recovery and security notices; (b) Consumer consent and connection records, and the evidence of them; (c) the prevention, detection and investigation of fraud, abuse, impersonation and security incidents; (d) tamper-evident audit logs and signed-decision records kept for dispute resolution; (e) billing, metering and accounting records; (f) compliance with Viadere's legal obligations; and (g) the operation, security and improvement of the Services using non-identifying technical data. Customer is an independent Controller for the Consumer Data it receives and holds. [COUNSEL: confirm the split, in particular for signed decisions, message delivery records and Verify.]

### 2.5 Duration

This DPA applies for as long as Viadere processes Customer Personal Data, and after the Agreement ends for as long as Viadere retains Customer Personal Data as section 11 allows.

## 3. Instructions

### 3.1 Documented instructions

Viadere will process Customer Personal Data only on Customer's documented instructions, which are this DPA, the Agreement, Customer's configuration of the Services (including API calls, webhook settings and retention settings) and any further written instructions that the parties agree are consistent with the Agreement. Viadere will tell Customer if, in its opinion, an instruction infringes Applicable Data Protection Law, and may suspend the instruction until Customer confirms or amends it.

### 3.2 Processing required by law

Viadere may process Customer Personal Data as required by law to which it is subject, in which case it will tell Customer before the processing, unless the law forbids it.

### 3.3 Additional instructions

If an instruction needs changes to the Services or goes beyond what the Services offer, Viadere may charge reasonable fees and may decline an instruction that is not technically feasible.

### 3.4 CCPA restrictions

Viadere will not: (a) Sell or Share Customer Personal Data; (b) retain, use or disclose Customer Personal Data for any purpose other than the business purposes specified in the Agreement and this DPA, including for a commercial purpose other than the business purposes, or outside the direct business relationship between Viadere and Customer, except as the CCPA allows; (c) combine Customer Personal Data with personal information that it receives from or on behalf of another person or collects from its own interactions with Consumers, except as the CCPA permits for the business purposes (for example, detecting security incidents and preventing fraud); or (d) otherwise process it in violation of the CCPA. Viadere certifies that it understands these restrictions and will comply with them. Viadere will notify Customer if it determines that it can no longer meet its obligations under the CCPA, and Customer may take reasonable and appropriate steps to stop and remediate unauthorized use. Customer may take reasonable steps to ensure that Viadere uses the personal information in a manner consistent with Customer's CCPA obligations, including by the audit rights in section 10.

## 4. Confidentiality and personnel

Viadere will ensure that persons it authorizes to process Customer Personal Data are bound by confidentiality duties (by contract or statute), receive appropriate training on data protection and security, and have access only to the extent necessary for their role under a least-privilege model. Access to production data is limited, requires strong authentication and is logged.

## 5. Security

### 5.1 Measures

Taking into account the state of the art, the costs of implementation and the nature, scope, context and purposes of the processing and the risks, Viadere will implement and maintain appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect Customer Personal Data, including those in Annex II. Viadere may update the measures, provided it does not materially decrease the overall security of the Services.

### 5.2 Customer's security responsibilities

Customer is responsible for its own use of the Services and for securing its credentials, endpoints, systems and devices, including by implementing the integration security requirements in the Documentation, and for assessing whether the measures in Annex II meet its needs.

### 5.3 Security Incidents

Viadere will notify Customer without undue delay and, where feasible, within [72] hours after becoming aware of a Security Incident, by email to Customer's security contact and in the Dashboard. The notice will include, to the extent known: a description of the nature of the incident, the categories and approximate number of Data Subjects and records, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed and a contact point. Where information is not available at once, Viadere will provide it in phases. Viadere will take reasonable steps to contain, investigate and remediate the Security Incident, and will cooperate with Customer to meet its obligations to notify authorities and Data Subjects. Notification is not an acknowledgment of fault or liability. Customer is responsible for deciding whether to notify authorities or Data Subjects for which it is the Controller. See the [Incident Notification Policy](/legal/incident-notification-policy/).

Taking into account the nature of the processing, Viadere will give Customer reasonable assistance, by appropriate technical and organizational measures, so that Customer can respond to requests from Data Subjects to exercise their rights. ### 8.2 Data protection impact assessments and consultations

On request, Viadere will give Customer information reasonably needed for it to carry out a data protection impact assessment or a prior consultation with a Supervisory Authority that relates to the Services, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to Viadere.

### 8.3 Regulator inquiries

Viadere will tell Customer promptly of an inquiry from a Supervisory Authority that relates to Customer Personal Data, unless the law prohibits it, and will cooperate as reasonably required.

### 8.4 Costs

### 11.2 After the Agreement ends

## Annex II. Technical and organizational measures

Viadere maintains at least the following. Items marked "planned" are not yet in place.

### 1. Governance and organization

- A designated security lead and a designated privacy lead with documented responsibilities.
- A secure software development lifecycle: reviewed changes, dependencies kept at the current stable release, security fixes that begin with a test reproducing the exploit, and releases from CI by signed image digest.
- A documented data inventory that classifies each table and states its protection and retention.
- Written runbooks for detection and response, including key compromise, business impersonation, webhook takeover, relay compromise and vendor outage, and tabletop exercises.
- A published vulnerability disclosure policy with patch targets by severity.

### 2. Access control and authentication

- Least-privilege roles, separation of duties and role-based access for Viadere's staff. Operator console access requires a passkey and, for sensitive actions, a fresh passkey assertion and, for some actions, two people.
- Customer Dashboard sessions use passkeys, expire, and are protected by CSRF checks. Dashboard roles are owner, admin, developer, support and analyst, and an optional security admin.
- Business API keys are 256-bit random values stored only as a hash, shown once, scoped, revocable, optionally expiring, bound to an IP allow-list or a client certificate, and rotated with overlap.
- Tenant isolation: every business request is scoped to the authenticated business, and a request for another business's record returns the same answer as a nonexistent record.
- Production access for engineers is limited, logged and reviewed.

### 3. Encryption and key management

- TLS for all connections to the Services. TLS 1.3 is preferred; TLS 1.2 is allowed only with ECDHE and authenticated-encryption suites under a documented policy. Connections to the database use TLS with certificate verification.
- Encryption at rest by the database and storage providers.
- Field-level encryption with AES-256-GCM, with additional data binding each ciphertext to its table, column and row, for phone numbers, message bodies, request details, webhook secrets, dashboard email addresses, support text and other sensitive fields.
- Tokens, API keys, recovery codes and one-time codes are stored only as keyed hashes.
- Service signing keys and the keys that wrap stored secrets are in Google Cloud KMS hardware-backed keys, with a dedicated service identity that may only sign and wrap or unwrap, and logged key use. [Planned: all long-lived service keys migrated behind KMS.]
- Separate keys for separate purposes (field encryption, hashing pepper, audit chain, pairwise references, link signing), and a documented rotation procedure.
- End-to-end sealing (HPKE, RFC 9180) of identity results to a business key, of approval requests in end-to-end mode and of Consumer data exports to a key held on the Consumer's device, with business keys published in an authenticated key directory.

### 4. Pseudonymization and data minimization

- A pairwise reference for each business and Consumer, derived with a dedicated secret key, so that a Consumer has unrelated references at different businesses and a business cannot compute or enumerate them.
- Business-facing audit views, exports and webhook bodies use only that business's reference. Device identifiers are never given to a business.
- No raw identity documents, selfies, birthdates or biometric templates are stored by Viadere. Verification evidence goes from the Consumer's device directly to the verification provider, and provider sessions are erased after the result is read.
- Business display text is validated to remove control characters and impersonation.

### 5. Integrity, availability and resilience

- A tamper-evident audit log with a keyed hash chain, and scheduled export of the chain head to write-once storage. Append-only database tables for audit events, signed decisions and consent records, with a narrow, audited retention exception.
- Replay protection, idempotency keys, rate limiting (shared across instances, failing closed for one-time-code and recovery paths) and abuse controls.
- At least two always-on application instances in production, rolling deploys with forward-only migrations, point-in-time database recovery (7 days) and daily snapshots (30 days), restore tests and documented recovery objectives.
- Operator kill switches by capability and by business.

### 6. Logging and monitoring

- Redacted server logs (tokens, secrets and phone numbers are removed), no message bodies in telemetry, centralized security logs with limited access and alerting on security events including key use and overdue provider purges.

### 7. Vulnerability management and testing

- Dependency and base-image refresh at least monthly and on security releases, secret scanning and static analysis in CI, signed and attested release artifacts, adversarial tests for each security fix and a documented patch schedule. [Planned: independent penetration test and cryptographic review before the Authenticator vault and Verify gates.]

### 8. Physical security

- Viadere operates no data centers. It relies on Subprocessors' facilities, which implement physical and environmental controls under their own programs, and Viadere reviews them as part of due diligence.

### 9. Personnel

- Confidentiality commitments, security awareness training and background checks as the law allows [COUNSEL AND HR: confirm].

### 10. Incident response

- A documented process for detection, containment, evidence preservation, notification and review, with notification periods stated in section 5.3, and independent contact paths that do not depend on Viadere's own systems.

### 11. Data deletion

- Automatic retention jobs by data class, account deletion that erases phone numbers, message bodies, interaction payloads and other personal data, and deletion of verification-provider sessions with retry until success.
